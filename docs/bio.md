# Osita Ogbu

**Osita Ogbu** (born 1957) is a Nigerian development economist, author, and former government official. He served as Chief Economic Adviser to the President of the Federal Republic of Nigeria and as Minister of National Planning from November 2005 to November 2006, under President Olusegun Obasanjo. He is a professor of economics at the University of Nigeria and Managing Director/CEO of African Development Solutions International (ADSI).

## Early life and background

Ogbu was born in Onitsha, Anambra State, Nigeria. He is from Ovoko in Igbo-Eze South Local Government Area, Enugu State. His early schooling was at Holy Trinity Primary School, Onitsha, and St. Teresa's College, Nsukka. Nsukka and Enugu have remained his institutional base: he took his first degree at the University of Nigeria, Nsukka, and has since been associated with the university and with research institutes in the area, including the Institute for Development Studies.

He trained as an economist. He holds a B.Sc. in Economics from the University of Nigeria, Nsukka (1979), an M.A. in Economics (December 1984) and a Ph.D. in Economics (May 1988) from Howard University, Washington D.C., and a Certificate in Corporate Governance from the Harvard Business School (2009).

## Academic career

After a research fellowship at Howard University (1985–1987), Ogbu worked as a consultant research economist at the World Bank (1987–1991) and as a senior program specialist at the International Development Research Centre (1991–2001). He was Executive Director/CEO of the African Technology Policy Studies Network from 2001 to 2005, and was a visiting fellow at the Brookings Institution in 2012. He has been a professor of economics at the Institute for Development Studies, University of Nigeria, Enugu Campus, and delivered the university's 133rd inaugural lecture in 2018.

## Government service

From November 2005 to November 2006, Ogbu served in the administration of President Olusegun Obasanjo as Chief Economic Adviser to the President and Minister of National Planning. This was his period of service as a presidential adviser. He did not hold an economic advisory appointment under President Goodluck Jonathan; he has, however, been a public commentator on economic management in Nigeria during that period.

## Later policy work and commentary

Ogbu chaired the Governing Council of the Nigerian Institute of Social and Economic Research (NISER) from 2008 to 2011. He has since served on a number of public bodies, including the boards of the Nigeria Deposit Insurance Corporation and Afrinvest (West Africa) Ltd, the governing council of VERITAS University, the Global Advisory Board of the Institute for Governance and Economic Transformation, the governing council of Enugu State University of Science and Technology, and the Economic Advisory Committee and Privatization Council of Enugu State. He leads African Development Solutions International.

Commenting on the administration of the Governor of Enugu State in May 2025, he said: "He is turning Enugu State into an entrepreneurial state, just like the transformations seen in China, Malaysia, and Israel under their visionary leaders." He added: "We are among the three states in the country that can say goodbye to Federal Allocation." Both remarks were reported by *Vanguard*, 11 May 2025, in "Enugu North stakeholders describe Mbah as a proactive, responsive leader".

### Views on education

Speaking at the Kaduna edition of the *Development as Attitude* National Dialogue Series in August 2025, Ogbu said that Nigerian universities had become degree-producing factories rather than problem-solving institutions, and warned that the country risked producing millions of unemployable graduates without urgent reform. In his words: "We think universities are set up to produce students, not to solve societal problems. That is one of our greatest crises." He contrasted this with institutions such as MIT, Harvard, and Yale in the United States, and the Hebrew University in Israel, which he described as central to solving national problems. The remarks were reported in *Blueprint*, 23 August 2025, in "Nigerian varsities churning out students, not solutions – Ex-minister Ogbu".

## Selected publications

- *The Moon Also Sets* (novel). East African Educational Publishers, Kenya / Heinemann Nigeria Ltd, Ibadan, 2002.
- *Development as Attitude: How National Progress Is Shaped by Leadership Philosophy and Citizens' Orientation*. Adonis & Abbey Publishers Ltd, London.
- Ogbu, Oyeyinka and Mlawa (eds.), *Technology Policy and Practice in Africa*. IDRC Books, 1995.
- Soludo, Ogbu and Chang (eds.), *The Politics of Trade and Industrial Policy in Africa: Forced Consensus*. Africa World Press / IDRC, 2004.
- Eboh and Ogbu (eds.), *The Global Economic Crisis and Nigeria*. African Institute of Applied Economics, 2010.
- Uramah et al., *The African Manifesto for Science, Technology and Innovation*. African Technology Policy Studies Network, 2010.

Some bibliographies list the author of *The Moon Also Sets* and of works co-edited with Charles C. Soludo as "Michael Osita Ogbu". Where the titles match, this is the same person.

## Honours

Ogbu was made a Fellow of the National Academy of Education and an Officer of the Order of the Niger.
